General

  • Safety — be very careful. A lot of pickpockets. People from my hostels got their phones stolen. Be careful pulling out your phone, avoid dangerous areas and be safe at night
    • In Rio: there are known incidents of flash mobs where 50+ people march down the beach and rob everyone one at a time. Police can't do much because of the numbers. If it happens, bury your valuables in the sand beforehand. Avoid Lapa at night — one guy from our hostel had his card stolen there the same night we were warned about it
  • Credit card works in big cities but bring cash for smaller ones
  • Must-eats: Brazilian BBQ, pão de queijo (cheese bread), açaí bowls and caipirinhas

Rio de Janeiro

3–4 days recommended
Where to Stay
  • Stay in Copacabana or Ipanema — the safest areas with beach access
  • More Ipanema Hostel — fun hostel with activities every day and a really cheap bar every night
What to Do
  • Christ the Redeemer — one of the seven wonders of the world. You can pay for a tram, hike up or take a heli ride around it. Really nice view of the entire city
  • Sugarloaf Mountain (Pão de Açucar) — take a tram up for another great view of the city. There's a bar on top and DJs sometimes play. Great for sunset
  • Hike Morro Dois Irmãos — my favorite hike in Brazil. Go to the bottom of a favela and take a motorcycle to the beginning of the route. The top has an amazing view of the city
    • Walk 45 minutes along the beach to the Vidigal favela, then take a mototaxi (7 reais, ~$1.40) up through the favela to the trailhead. First viewpoint faces south: the entire favela valley of tiny houses stacked into the hillside, somehow mesmerizing. Second viewpoint faces north: same Vidigal valley to the left, Christ the Redeemer, and the full coastline. Workers hanging from wires high above — birds flying below them. The favela felt similar to other poorer South American cities, less intimidating than expected
  • Hike Pedra do Telegrafo — another awesome hike with the instafamous fake hanging rock. Take a taxi a bit outside of the city. Really nice view at the top
    • About 2.3 miles total. The famous hanging photo spot: there's a rock at the top where you appear to be hanging off a cliff, but you're literally 2 inches off the ground. Massive queue — two middle-age ladies took 20 minutes each posing, whole crowd clapped sarcastically when they finished. Condors circle overhead. Motorcycle ride down from the trailhead is the sketchiest part — drivers go fast, cut close to buildings
  • Escadaria Selarón — famous colorful stairs. Mainly a place to take photos and get a cheap caipirinha
  • Olympic Boulevard / Museum of Tomorrow — some nice murals and a cool looking museum
  • Parque Lage — nice palace with a cafe in the middle of the city
  • Copacabana / Ipanema Beach — just chill for a day and walk around

Ilha Grande

2–3 days recommended — 2 hours from Rio

Beautiful island. Book a transfer — cheap and covers both the van and boat ride.

What to Do
  • Snorkeling — some of the best snorkeling I've seen. Tons of fish and some turtles if you swim away from the crowd
    • Lagoa Azul: super clear blue water, hundreds of fish including zebra fish and a sea turtle within a couple feet if you swim to the far corner away from the main group. Lagoa Verde: strange clear green water matching the island's trees, hundreds of fish literally touching your legs when you're in the middle of them, another turtle that wasn't scared and I followed for 10 minutes. Also spotted baby angler fish — completely black with 5 yellow stripes. The density of fish here compared to anywhere else I snorkeled was genuinely shocking
    • Hike through the forest, see waterfalls and go to remote beaches

Paraty

1–2 days recommended

Nice quiet colonial town with snorkeling.

  • The old town has white buildings with bright colored door frames (red, yellow, blue, green) — distinct from Spanish colonial towns. Huge cobblestones, feels like a slice of Europe. Most buildings are not in active use despite being remodeled outside. The town rose during the gold rush as a transit route between Rio and São Paulo, then lost relevance when a highway was built, population dropped to 600 people, and was only reconnected by road in the 1970s
  • Best food: Manue Sucos — beef and cheese Dobrado (lavash wrap with shredded beef and cheese, first Brazilian food I actually loved). Their mango and açaí shake is good too
Where to Stay
  • Selina Paraty — nice hostel, really chill

São Paulo

2–3 days recommended

Big urban metropolis without much historic stuff but worth a couple of days.

  • Crime is real — someone from my hostel got robbed at gunpoint outside the front door at 6am waiting for an Uber. Stay aware, especially early mornings
Where to Stay
  • O Casa Hostel — really good social vibe, lots of activities and a bar at night
What to Do
  • Beco do Batman — alley with cool graffiti
    • Started as a single mural and organically grew into an entire alley. Well done and genuinely cool — not just tourist wallpaper. About 20 minutes to walk through properly
  • Walking tour of the downtown — shows you the famous buildings and history
Where to Eat
  • Galinheiro Grill — my favorite restaurant in Brazil. Amazing steak and sides for not too expensive
    • Order the baby beef — it comes out as a genuinely enormous amount of steak (more than 2 lbs) with rice, beans and fried polenta. I ate half and had enough for dinner the next night

Iguazu Falls

2 days recommended

One of the seven natural wonders of the world. Worth doing 1 day on the Brazilian side and 1 day on the Argentinian side.

  • Brazilian side: cement walkways through the forest to platform views of the falls from a distance. The falls stretch for miles side by side — the upper river flows peacefully then drops dramatically. The surrounding forest feels like Avatar
  • Argentinian side boat tour: they drive the boat directly into the falls and ram the bow into the water wall multiple times. Completely soaked, you'll shake water out of your sleeves afterwards. Bring your GoPro
  • Brazilian side gives you the view from farther away. Argentinian side is closer
  • On the Argentinian side, take the boat tour that drives straight into the waterfalls — highly recommend
