General

Georgia is one of the most underrated countries in the world and is starting to become very popular in the backpacker community. The small country is located in the Southern Caucuses lodged between Russia, Turkey and it's other Caucasian neighbors Armenia and Azerbaijan.

Georgia hosts jaw-dropping mountain scenery, ancient churches as the first Christian nation in the world, an incredible cuisine and Soviet Era history all packed into a small country. It's safe, affordable and welcoming to foreigners, yet still nowhere near as touristed as it deserves to be.

  • Getting around
    • Flights — there are non-stop flights to Tbilisi from 32 countries including most major European cities and key hubs like Istanbul, Dubai and Qatar
    • Ridesharing — Bolt and Yandex both work well for taxis and are super cheap. A 30-minute ride can run you less than 15 Georgian Lari or $6 USD
    • Public Transport — despite how small the country is, it takes surprising long to get around. The country is covered in mountainous terrain and
      • Shared Marshrutka (Minibus) — the cheapest way to get around Georgia is the "Marshrutka" or a shared Minibus that leaves when it is full. Find times online on Wander Lush's Transport Tips section (the absolute best travel blog on Georgia), marshrutka.info, or ask a local! Budget travel time with Marshrutkas as they aren't known for their timelines
      • Trains — Georgia has a train network connecting it's major cities. There are a mix of ultra modern trains that were built in the last few years and old Soviet Trains that move at a snails pace. View schedules online at the official Georgian Railway website
    • GoTrip — a unique private taxi service that is surprisingly affordable and allows you unlimited stops along the way. I used this service to from Kutaisi to Tbilisi and made 4 stops along the way. I highly recommend this option if you're short on time and want to pack your day with activities. Expect to pay ~$100 per day
    • Day Tours — these are a great way to avoid travel logistics and there are many that go to Georgia's more remote mountains regions like Kazbegi or Svaneti. Tours are generally very affordable from $20 - $30
  • Food — Georgian food is incredible, unlike the food I've had elsewhere and one of the best reasons to come. Here are a few of the highlights:
    • Khinkali — large soup dumplings stuffed with lamb and beef. Grab them by the topknot, bite a hole, sip the broth
    • Khachapuri — a bread boat of with pool of cheese and an egg cracked on top. Break off the pieces of the bread and dip them in the cheese to eat them
    • Kubdari (or "Svan Pie") — a large meat pie stuffed with tenderized beef or pork, onions, garlic, and spices
    • Wine — Georgia is considered the birthplace of wine and still uses its own ancient method of fermenting in clay pots (qvevri) buried in the ground. The amber "orange" wines are especially unique and the main type of wine they're famous for
    • Chacha — is the local grape brandy which is very strong at 40% to 60% ABV. It is rude to refuse Chacha when it is offered to you and may be the reasons why Georgians drink the second most in the world!
  • Language — Georgian has its own beautiful alphabet. There is tremendous ethnic diversity within Georgians themselves so there are 4 different Georgian languages with many local dialects on top of that. A lot of Georgians know Russian and English is common enough with younger people
  • Money — Georgia is extremely affordable for tourists. I found Georgians also extremely fair with no "tourist tax" or scams that you'll find in other countries
  • Safety — Georgia today is very safe and the country has made tremendous strides from the 1990's when it struggled with organized crime. However, Georgia has continuously had territorial disputes with separatist groups supported by Russia. Over 20% of it's land is occupied since the 2008 Russo-Georgian War. When I visited, these areas were very well defined and fortified so there wasn't any fear of another conflict breaking out
  • How to long to stay — for first timers, I'd recommend at least 10 to 14 days to thoroughly explore the country. You can also combine Georgia with a trip to Armenia (read my guide here) which is only a three hour bus ride or overnight train from Tbilisi

Tbilisi

3 days recommended and add on additional days for day trips

Tbilisi is one of my favorite cities in the world. It is the type of place where you could just spend days wandering or relaxing in a cafe. Officially founded in the 5th century AD, Tbilisi is one of the world's oldest cities and important hub on the Silk Road.Ancient churches, Soviet brutalism, ultra-modern sculptures and great restaurants are all within walking distance of each other.

I would dedicate at least 3 days to exploring the city. It is also a great place to base yourself for day tours around the country so add on extra days here for each day tour.

What to Do
  • Old Town (Sololaki) — super pretty, with cobblestone streets, carved wooden balconies and ancient churches tucked everywhere. Best in the morning before the crowds arrive
    • Clock Tower — the leaning clock tower covered in little tile paintings
    • Queen Darejan Palace balcony — a really pretty garden with a wide balcony overlooking the river
    • Dry Bridge Flea Market — old Soviet items, art and random antiques. Great for browsing, daily
    • Holy Trinity Cathedral (Sameba) — an enormous Georgian cathedral with a gold dome that towers over the whole city. The courtyard with huge carved crosses is impressive too
    • Anchiskhati Basilica — one of the oldest churches in Tbilisi. Small, dark, flickering candles; a polyphonic choir performs at 10AM
    • St. Abo Tbileli Church — a small one-room church on the riverfront near Europe Square, with two side chapels and golden frescos. Almost no one there
  • Rike Park — a modern riverside park with bizarre alien-shaped sculptures and the wavy "Peace Bridge." A cool contrast to the old town right across the water. The beginning of the cable car up to the next location called Sololaki Street
  • Sololaki Street — located on a hill overlooking old town. This is a great place to spend the afternoon and visit all the attractions on top of the hill. You can hike up here or take the cable car from Rike Park
    • Mother of Georgia
    • Tabor Monastery viewpoint
    • Betlemi Rise viewpoint — a great place to watch the sunset over old town
    • National Botanical Garden
  • Soviet-era relics — Tbilisi is full of them, and hunting them down is half the fun:
    • Chronicles of Georgia — 20 minutes from the center but worth it. A massive, unfinished Soviet monument covered in metal biblical reliefs — like Egyptian hieroglyphic pillars, but Soviet. Mesmerizing, and almost no tourists
    • Soviet Mosaics
      • Hunt for Soviet Relics — Former Laboratory of the Ministry of Forestry — nearby in Saburtalo, with a stunning 5-story "Tree of Life" mosaic on the exterior
      • Emergency Management mural — an impressive Soviet-era mosaic on the side of the active fire station by the central bus station. Stand in the parking lot next door for the best view
    • Brutalist Architecture — I know brutalist architecture is an acquired taste (and not for everyone). Tbilisi is home to some of the most famous examples including
      • Nutsubidze Skybridge — a checkered skybridge connecting connecting two massive Soviet-Era apartment towers. Take the original coin-operated elevator and admire the striking geometric shapes. It feels completely forgotten by time. Located a short car ride from old town
      • Palace of Rituals and Bank of Georgia are also famous Brutalist architecture sites in Tbilisi
    • Fabrika — a converted Soviet clothing factory that has been remodeled into a street art area. There is a hostel, coffee shop, hip clothing shops, bars and street art. A great stop for a morning coffee
  • Georgian National Museum — the national museum with the history of Georgia from prehistoric to modern times. The Soviet occupation hall is worth your time
Where to Eat
  • Cafe Daphna — upscale but worth it. The beef khinkali are some of the best in the city
  • Puri Galiani — get the khachapuri; an unreal portion of bread and cheese
  • Pasanauri — try the deer meat cooked with pomegranate seeds, served with bread and the house Georgian beer
  • Racha — a solid, no-frills spot for khinkali, popular with locals
  • Sasadilo Canteen — hidden inside a warehouse market near the main train station. An adventure to find, but worth it — bread with dips and cheese dumplings in a herb-oil sauce
  • Amra — Abkhazian cuisine, unique regional dishes that are hard to find elsewhere in Georgia
Where to Drink
  • Shavi Coffee Roasters — the best coffee in the city, in a super trendy space inside an old building
  • Apotheka — a popular bar in old town
  • Wine Factory N1 — a good spot to work through a range of Georgian wines
  • Graminea — a wine shop in old town with a great selection. A relaxed place to sit and try a few glasses of Georgian wine
Where to stay
  • Envoy Hostel Tbilisi — a hostel located right in Old Town Tbilisi, with an amazing rooftop overlooking the city. It is walking distance from all major historical sites. They also offer a number of affordable tours including unique one-way transfer tour to Yerevan from Tbilisi with a lot of interesting stops along the way

    Kazbegi Day Trip

    Day trip from Tbilisi

    One of the most beautiful drives you'll ever do. Book a day tour from Tbilisi — multiple operators run them and they fill up fast. The drive through the Georgian countryside alone is worth the trip: snow-peaked mountains, green valleys, herds of horses and sheep, and lines of trucks waiting weeks to cross into Russia.

    What to Do
    • Zhinvali Reservoir & Ananuri — the first stop on the way. An ancient church from the Georgian golden age perched over a turquoise reservoir, with castle walls you can climb
    • Gergeti Trinity Church — the main event. A medieval church in the clouds, surrounded by mountains and impossibly green valleys — it looks like a fantasy world, and it's only 15km from Russia
    • Georgian-Russian Friendship Monument — a circular fresco on top of a mountain pass, Georgian warriors on one side and Soviet soldiers on the other. Pretty, and a completely bizarre location in the middle of nowhere

    Kakheti Wine Region

    Day trip from Tbilisi

    Georgia's wine country, an easy day trip east of Tbilisi. Rolling vineyards, hilltop towns and family wineries still fermenting in buried clay pots.

    What to Do
    • Traditional bread demo — most tours start at a small family house where an old woman makes Georgian bread in a wood-fired oven, pressing the dough straight onto the walls without gloves. You eat it in the garden with salty Georgian cheese and sweet red "breakfast wine"
    • Bodbe Monastery — a beautiful nunnery overlooking a huge valley, with two chapels (one modern, one from the Georgian golden age). The garden is gorgeous
    • Sighnaghi — a small "city of love" perched on a hilltop with castle walls wrapping right around it. Walk the walls for great views over the valley
      • Find the Toastmaster (Tamada) statue in the main park — in Georgian culture the tamada gives a toast and everyone chugs a liter of wine from a horn. Supposedly touching the statue gives you better chugging ability
    • Family winery in Kvareli — the most interesting stop. You see the ancient Georgian method of fermenting wine in clay pots buried in the ground; try the orange wines. A chacha tasting is usually included — a lemon one and a couple of herb-infused ones. Strong

    Svaneti (Mestia & Ushguli)

    3+ days · fly or full-day drive from Tbilisi

    The highlight of Georgia and one of the most beautiful places I've ever been — a remote, high-mountain region of medieval stone towers, wildflower valleys and glaciers. Give it at least 2–3 days.

    What to Do
    • Mestia town — a tiny mountain town studded with Svan defensive towers. Walk the river, wander the side streets and just take it all in
    • Mikheil Khergiani House Museum — dedicated to a legendary Soviet climber. Climb to the roof of the tower for one of the best views in the whole region; the stairs are sketchy but worth it
    • Chalaadi Glacier hike — a 4WD ride to the trailhead, then a ~45-minute hike through forest and red/white rocks to the glacier
    • Ushguli — 1.5 hours of extremely broken road from Mestia, but one of the most beautiful places I've ever visited: medieval villages in a valley of towers, wildflowers, roaming horses and huge white mountains
      • Queen Tamar's Tower — the best viewpoint, overlooking both villages and the mountains behind
      • Lamaria Church — a small church on a flower-covered hill; the surrounding hillside is stunning
    Where to Eat
    • Cafe Laila (Mestia) — get the Kubdari (pita stuffed with beef and onion) and a mulled wine
    • Inna's Corner (Mestia) — a tiny place run by an old woman and her daughter. Great for breakfast
    • Agra Cafe (Ushguli) — try the matsoni cheese soup and a glass of orange wine

    Other Stops

    Georgia is small and packed, so it's easy to string together more stops depending on how much time you have:

    • Chiatura — an old Soviet mining town nestled in a valley so tight you can't see it until you're inside it. Super atmospheric, all river, rusty roofs and Soviet apartment blocks
      • Cable cars — locals use these as actual public transport. Take the modern one up for the view, then try the old retro one with bubble windows
      • The mines — at the top there's an enormous abandoned excavator, still with the original hard hats and papers inside
    • Gori — Stalin's hometown, about an hour from Tbilisi
      • Stalin Museum — a surprisingly well-curated (if uncritical) museum; his office from the Kremlin and his private train car are the highlights. Get the guided tour. As a young man he robbed banks to fund the communist party, and there are rumors he had his wife, Lenin and Trotsky killed — he was apparently the first "photoshopped" person, insecure about his arms being different lengths
      • Gori Fortress — broken down, but a decent view of the city. The nearby Memorial of Georgian War Heroes is a striking WWII monument — a circle of knights each missing a body part, an intentional artistic choice
    • Mtskheta — the ancient capital, at the meeting of three rivers and only 20 minutes from Tbilisi
      • Svetitskhoveli Cathedral — one of the largest and most impressive churches in Georgia, and locals actively pray here
      • Jvari Monastery — built around 500–600 AD on a hilltop, with an amazing view over the confluence of the three rivers
    • Katskhi Pillar (near Kutaisi) — a tiny monastery perched on top of a near-vertical column of rock in the middle of nowhere. Best viewed from the base in the morning before any other tourists show up
