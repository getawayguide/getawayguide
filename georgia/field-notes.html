General

Georgia is one of the most underrated countries in the world — ancient churches, Soviet monuments, incredible food and jaw-dropping mountain scenery all packed into a small country. It's very safe, very cheap and very welcoming to foreigners, and still nowhere near as touristed as it deserves to be.

  • Getting around — Bolt and Yandex both work well for taxis and are super cheap; a 30-minute ride can cost less than $6. For the mountains (Kazbegi, Kakheti, Svaneti), book a day tour or a shared marshrutka (minibus)
  • Food — Georgian food is incredible and one of the best reasons to come. A few must-tries:
    • Khinkali — soup dumplings; grab them by the topknot, bite a hole, sip the broth
    • Khachapuri — cheese bread, in a dozen regional forms (the Adjarian one is a boat of cheese with an egg cracked on top)
    • Anything with walnut sauce — it turns up everywhere and it's always good
  • Wine — a huge deal here. Georgia is considered the birthplace of wine and still uses its own ancient method of fermenting in clay pots (qvevri) buried in the ground. The amber "orange" wines are especially unique. Chacha is the local grape brandy — stronger than it looks
  • Language — Georgian has its own beautiful alphabet. A lot of Georgians know Russian but may refuse to speak it for political reasons; English is common enough with younger people
  • Money — extremely affordable. The average wage is around $400/month, so tipping well goes a long way
  • Safety — Georgia feels very safe. The government cleaned up organized crime in the mid-2000s by giving all the mob bosses 48 hours to leave the country or face 7 years in jail — even once released they had 24 hours to leave again. The country went from genuinely dangerous to safe almost overnight (our guide compared it to what Bukele is doing in El Salvador). One real caveat: the stray dogs. I got surrounded by six of them near the central bus station and had to sprint to a random government building — they probably wouldn't have bitten, but it got the adrenaline going

Tbilisi

3+ days recommended

Give the capital at least 3 days. Tbilisi rewards wandering — old-town churches, Soviet brutalism, modern sculptures and great restaurants are all within walking distance of each other, and the city looks completely different depending on which hillside you're standing on.

What to Do
  • Old Town / Sololaki — super pretty, with cobblestone streets, carved wooden balconies and ancient churches tucked everywhere. Best in the morning before the crowds arrive
  • Anchiskhati Basilica — one of the oldest churches in Tbilisi. Small, dark, flickering candles; a polyphonic choir performs at 10AM
  • Holy Trinity Cathedral (Sameba) — an enormous Georgian cathedral with a gold dome that towers over the whole city. The courtyard with huge carved crosses is impressive too
  • Narikala Fortress & Mother of Georgia — the castle overlooking old town. Take the cable car up from Rike Park; lit up at night it looks magical
  • Clock Tower — the leaning clock tower covered in little tile paintings, right next to Anchiskhati Basilica
  • Rike Park — a modern riverside park with bizarre alien-shaped sculptures and the wavy "Peace Bridge." A cool contrast to the old town right across the water
  • St. Abo Tbileli Church — a small one-room church on the riverfront near Europe Square, with two side chapels and golden frescos. Almost no one there
  • Queen Darejan Palace balcony — a really pretty garden with a wide balcony overlooking the far side of the river. Again, almost no one there
  • Dry Bridge Flea Market — old Soviet items, art and random antiques. Great for browsing
  • Fabrika — a converted Soviet clothing factory, now full of hip shops, bars and street art. Good grungy creative energy
  • Georgian National Museum — a good overview of Georgian history (the Soviet occupation hall is worth your time)
  • Soviet-era relics — Tbilisi is full of them, and hunting them down is half the fun:
    • Chronicles of Georgia — 20 minutes from the center but worth it. A massive, unfinished Soviet monument covered in metal biblical reliefs — like Egyptian hieroglyphic pillars, but Soviet. Mesmerizing, and almost no tourists
    • Nutsubidze Skybridge (Saburtalo) — a hardcore Soviet apartment complex with a checkered skybridge connecting the towers. Take the original coin-operated elevator; it feels completely forgotten by time
    • Former Laboratory of the Ministry of Forestry — nearby in Saburtalo, with a stunning 5-story "Tree of Life" mosaic on the exterior
    • Emergency Management mural — an impressive Soviet-era mosaic on the side of the active fire station by the central bus station. Stand in the parking lot next door for the best view
  • Betlemi Rise viewpoint — the best sunset view over old town. Tabor Monastery viewpoint and King Parnavaz Garden are also great for golden hour
Where to Eat
  • Cafe Daphna — upscale but worth it. The beef khinkali are some of the best in the city
  • Puri Galiani — get the khachapuri; an unreal portion of bread and cheese
  • Pasanauri — try the deer meat cooked with pomegranate seeds, served with bread and the house Georgian beer
  • Racha — a solid, no-frills spot for khinkali, popular with locals
  • Sasadilo Canteen — hidden inside a warehouse market near the main train station. An adventure to find, but worth it — bread with dips and cheese dumplings in a herb-oil sauce
  • Amra — Abkhazian cuisine; unique regional dishes you won't find elsewhere
  • Shavi Coffee Roasters — the best coffee in the city, in a super trendy space inside an old building
Where to Drink
  • Apotheka — a popular bar in old town
  • Wine Factory N1 — a good spot to work through a range of Georgian wines
  • Graminea — a wine shop in old town with a great selection. A much more relaxed place to sit and try a few glasses than a bar

Kazbegi Day Trip

Day trip from Tbilisi

One of the most beautiful drives you'll ever do. Book a day tour from Tbilisi — multiple operators run them and they fill up fast. The drive through the Georgian countryside alone is worth the trip: snow-peaked mountains, green valleys, herds of horses and sheep, and lines of trucks waiting weeks to cross into Russia.

What to Do
  • Zhinvali Reservoir & Ananuri — the first stop on the way. An ancient church from the Georgian golden age perched over a turquoise reservoir, with castle walls you can climb
  • Gergeti Trinity Church — the main event. A medieval church in the clouds, surrounded by mountains and impossibly green valleys — it looks like a fantasy world, and it's only 15km from Russia
  • Georgian-Russian Friendship Monument — a circular fresco on top of a mountain pass, Georgian warriors on one side and Soviet soldiers on the other. Pretty, and a completely bizarre location in the middle of nowhere

Kakheti Wine Region

Day trip from Tbilisi

Georgia's wine country, an easy day trip east of Tbilisi. Rolling vineyards, hilltop towns and family wineries still fermenting in buried clay pots.

What to Do
  • Traditional bread demo — most tours start at a small family house where an old woman makes Georgian bread in a wood-fired oven, pressing the dough straight onto the walls without gloves. You eat it in the garden with salty Georgian cheese and sweet red "breakfast wine"
  • Bodbe Monastery — a beautiful nunnery overlooking a huge valley, with two chapels (one modern, one from the Georgian golden age). The garden is gorgeous
  • Sighnaghi — a small "city of love" perched on a hilltop with castle walls wrapping right around it. Walk the walls for great views over the valley
    • Find the Toastmaster (Tamada) statue in the main park — in Georgian culture the tamada gives a toast and everyone chugs a liter of wine from a horn. Supposedly touching the statue gives you better chugging ability
  • Family winery in Kvareli — the most interesting stop. You see the ancient Georgian method of fermenting wine in clay pots buried in the ground; try the orange wines. A chacha tasting is usually included — a lemon one and a couple of herb-infused ones. Strong

Svaneti (Mestia & Ushguli)

3+ days · fly or full-day drive from Tbilisi

The highlight of Georgia and one of the most beautiful places I've ever been — a remote, high-mountain region of medieval stone towers, wildflower valleys and glaciers. Give it at least 2–3 days.

What to Do
  • Mestia town — a tiny mountain town studded with Svan defensive towers. Walk the river, wander the side streets and just take it all in
  • Mikheil Khergiani House Museum — dedicated to a legendary Soviet climber. Climb to the roof of the tower for one of the best views in the whole region; the stairs are sketchy but worth it
  • Chalaadi Glacier hike — a 4WD ride to the trailhead, then a ~45-minute hike through forest and red/white rocks to the glacier
  • Ushguli — 1.5 hours of extremely broken road from Mestia, but one of the most beautiful places I've ever visited: medieval villages in a valley of towers, wildflowers, roaming horses and huge white mountains
    • Queen Tamar's Tower — the best viewpoint, overlooking both villages and the mountains behind
    • Lamaria Church — a small church on a flower-covered hill; the surrounding hillside is stunning
Where to Eat
  • Cafe Laila (Mestia) — get the kubdari (pita stuffed with beef and onion) and a mulled wine
  • Inna's Corner (Mestia) — a tiny place run by an old woman and her daughter. Great for breakfast
  • Agra Cafe (Ushguli) — try the matsoni cheese soup and a glass of orange wine

Other Stops

Georgia is small and packed, so it's easy to string together more stops depending on how much time you have:

  • Chiatura — an old Soviet mining town nestled in a valley so tight you can't see it until you're inside it. Super atmospheric, all river, rusty roofs and Soviet apartment blocks
    • Cable cars — locals use these as actual public transport. Take the modern one up for the view, then try the old retro one with bubble windows
    • The mines — at the top there's an enormous abandoned excavator, still with the original hard hats and papers inside
  • Gori — Stalin's hometown, about an hour from Tbilisi
    • Stalin Museum — a surprisingly well-curated (if uncritical) museum; his office from the Kremlin and his private train car are the highlights. Get the guided tour. As a young man he robbed banks to fund the communist party, and there are rumors he had his wife, Lenin and Trotsky killed — he was apparently the first "photoshopped" person, insecure about his arms being different lengths
    • Gori Fortress — broken down, but a decent view of the city. The nearby Memorial of Georgian War Heroes is a striking WWII monument — a circle of knights each missing a body part, an intentional artistic choice
  • Mtskheta — the ancient capital, at the meeting of three rivers and only 20 minutes from Tbilisi
    • Svetitskhoveli Cathedral — one of the largest and most impressive churches in Georgia, and locals actively pray here
    • Jvari Monastery — built around 500–600 AD on a hilltop, with an amazing view over the confluence of the three rivers
  • Katskhi Pillar (near Kutaisi) — a tiny monastery perched on top of a near-vertical column of rock in the middle of nowhere. Best viewed from the base in the morning before any other tourists show up
