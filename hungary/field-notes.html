General

Hungary sits in the heart of Central Europe, and for most travelers the country is really about one city: Budapest. Split by the Danube into hilly Buda and flat Pest, it's a grand, slightly faded imperial capital known for its thermal baths, ruin bars, and some of the best river views in Europe.

Budapest is easy to reach and easy to combine with Vienna or the rest of Central Europe. Most people spend their time in the city, but the lakes and small towns beyond it are worth adding on if you have extra days.

  • Getting around — Budapest's airport (BUD) connects to most of Europe. The city has an excellent metro, tram and bus network, and the center is compact enough to walk much of it. Trains link Budapest to Vienna, Bratislava and beyond in a few hours
  • Money — Hungary is in the EU but not the Eurozone, so it uses the Hungarian Forint (HUF). It's noticeably cheaper than Western Europe. Cards are widely accepted, but keep some cash for markets and ruin bars
  • Food — hearty and paprika-heavy: goulash, chicken paprikás and lángos (fried dough). Save room for the historic coffee houses and strudel
  • Language — Hungarian (Magyar) is unlike any of its neighbors' languages and famously hard, but English is common in Budapest and tourist areas
  • Safety — Budapest felt very safe day and night; just watch for the usual tourist-area pickpocketing and overpriced taxis
  • How long to stay — 4 to 5 days is enough for Budapest with time to slow down. Add more if you want to reach Lake Balaton or the Danube Bend

Budapest

4–5 days recommended

Budapest is two former cities facing each other across the Danube: hilly, historic Buda on the west bank and flat, grand Pest on the east. It's a city built for wandering, with imperial architecture, thermal baths, and a nightlife scene centered on its famous ruin bars.

What to Do
  • Széchenyi Chain Bridge — the most beautiful bridge in Budapest, connecting Buda and Pest across the Danube. Walk across it; it's perfectly photogenic from Buda Castle hill
  • Buda Castle — a palace complex on the hill (despite the name). It's less impressive inside than from across the river; the views over the city and Parliament are the main draw, and the whole castle district is worth an hour of wandering
  • Fisherman's Bastion — a neo-Romanesque terrace with 8 towers overlooking the river, with a café embedded in one tower. The views of Parliament and the river are among the best in the city; free to walk the lower terrace
  • Matthias Church (Mátyás-templom) — right next to Fisherman's Bastion, strikingly decorated both inside and out. The patterned colored-tile roof is its signature
  • Hungarian Parliament — across the Danube in Pest, one of the most intricate buildings anywhere in the world. Take a guided tour inside; the night view from across the river is spectacular
  • House of Terror — a museum in the former headquarters of the Nazi Arrow Cross and later the ÁVO (Hungarian secret police). The audio guide is excellent, covering the Fascist occupation and then the Communist terror in vivid detail. One of the most powerful museums of the trip
  • Danube river cruise — a 1.5-hour evening cruise (some include unlimited Prosecco) that passes all the major monuments including Parliament. Very good value
  • Széchenyi Thermal Baths (Sparty) — an outdoor thermal bath party on Saturday evenings with DJs, fire performers and dancers, across three pools (two hot, one cooler). One of the most memorable nights of the trip; buy tickets in advance
Where to Eat
  • Goulash — the Hungarian signature dish, a thick paprika-and-beef soup. Order it anywhere; try Horgásztanya Vendéglő near the hotel district
  • Chicken paprikás with bread dumplings — the best dish I had in Budapest: a creamy paprika chicken sauce with bread dumplings that soak it all up. Incredible
  • Norma Scandinavian Bakery (Central Market) — strawberry cheese strudel and coffee; excellent
  • Central Market Hall — a 3-story market with meat, spice, produce and souvenir stalls. Good for a breakfast browse; grab fresh orange juice at the ground-level stalls
Where to Drink
  • Szimpla Kert — the original Budapest ruin bar, in the Jewish Quarter. Two stories and many rooms, each with a different theme and music; a maze of mismatched furniture and art. An essential Budapest experience
  • Instant-Fogas — a nearby, slightly larger ruin bar with many themed rooms. Worth a wander even if you just walk through all the spaces
  • Mazel Tov — a wine bar with vines and fairy lights overhead in the Jewish Quarter. Beautiful and romantic; good for cocktails before a big night
Where to Stay
  • Monastery Boutique Hotel — a boutique hotel set in a former monastery, centrally located in Pest and within walking distance of the main sights

Other Places to Consider

Almost everyone sticks to Budapest, but if you have extra days, Hungary has plenty more worth adding on:

  • Lake Balaton — Central Europe's largest lake and Hungary's summer beach destination, a couple of hours southwest of Budapest
  • Szentendre — a small riverside art town on the Danube Bend, an easy day trip from Budapest
  • Eger — a historic town known for its castle, baroque streets and the "Bull's Blood" red wine of the Valley of the Beautiful Women
  • Danube Bend — a scenic curve of the river north of Budapest, with hilltop castles at Visegrád and Hungary's largest basilica at Esztergom
  • Pécs — a warm southern city with early Christian tombs, Ottoman-era architecture and a lively student scene
