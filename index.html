Getting There

Google Flights

Free

Still the best way to scout flight prices. I use the explore map to find where's cheap from my city at any given time.

Skyscanner

Free

Better than Google Flights for obscure budget carriers and multi-city routes in less common regions.

Visit →

Rome2Rio

Free

Invaluable for overland travel — shows every way to get between two points including trains, buses, ferries and driving time.

Visit →

Seat61

Free

The definitive guide to train and ferry travel worldwide. Written by one obsessive person. More reliable than any official rail website.

Visit →

Booking.com

Free

My default for hotels and guesthouses. The free cancellation filter is the most useful feature — book early, cancel if plans change.

Free

Better hostel inventory than Booking. The review system is more detailed and I trust it more for solo travel.

Useful for apartments on longer stays, particularly in Eastern Europe and Latin America where it tends to be better value.

Free

Offline maps that work without data. I download every country before I arrive. Saved me more times than I can count.

The person behind the passport

Hello, I'm Your Name.

I left a desk job in 2022 with a one-way ticket and a notebook. What started as a sabbatical turned into something I couldn't stop. Three years, 49 countries, and counting.

Getawayguide is where I write about what I actually found — the gap between the Instagram version of a place and the real one. Slow travel, deep stories.

49Countries visited
4Years on the road
6Continents explored
1Bag. Always carry-on.

How this started

I used to work in [industry]. Good job, good salary, the whole thing. But I kept noticing that the weeks I spent abroad — even short trips — felt more alive than everything else combined. So in early 2022, I handed in my notice and booked a flight to Lisbon with no return date.

The plan was six months. Three years later I'm still going, albeit more slowly. I've learned that the way I like to travel is incompatible with itineraries that try to fit six cities into ten days. I'd rather spend a month in one place and actually understand something about it.

How I travel

Almost always solo. Almost always overland when possible — I've taken more buses than I can count, a lot of slow overnight trains, a few cargo boats. I stay in a mix of hostels, guesthouses, and Workaway placements. I try to eat where locals eat and avoid the menu with photos.

The blog is a record of that. Not a highlight reel. I write about the things that go wrong as much as the things that go right, and I try to be honest about what a place is really like to travel in — costs, safety, logistics, the things the travel guides smooth over.

What I'm working on

Right now I'm slowly building out the destination guides — each country page will eventually have a full write-up with itineraries, logistics, and recommended reads. It's a long project. Subscribe to the newsletter if you want to know when new ones land.
