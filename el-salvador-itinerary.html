El Salvador Travel Guide with Perfect 1-2 Week Itinerary

Everything you need to plan a backpacking trip through El Salvador, from volcanoes and colonial towns to surf beaches and hot springs

Mar 28, 2026

El Salvador

El Salvador is one of Central America's best kept secrets. It's tiny — you can drive coast to coast in under three hours — but it packs in volcanoes, surf beaches, colonial towns, and cloud forest all within reach of each other. Here's how I'd spend one to two weeks if I were doing it again.

Before you go: El Salvador is part of the CA-4 agreement. Your 90-day visa-free allowance is shared across Guatemala, Honduras, and Nicaragua — so if you're combining countries, keep track of your days.

[Photo]

When to Visit

The best time to visit El Salvador is during the dry season, which runs from November through April. Skies are clear, roads are accessible, and the surf is consistent along the Pacific coast. The rainy season (May–October) brings lush green landscapes and far fewer tourists — a worthwhile trade-off if you don’t mind the occasional afternoon downpour. December and January are the busiest months, so book accommodation early if you’re travelling then.

[Photo]

Places to Visit

El Salvador packs a surprising amount into a small country. Suchitoto is a beautifully preserved colonial town perched above Lago Suchitlán, perfect for a slow day of cobblestone wandering. The Ruta de las Flores winds through flower-draped mountain villages with excellent coffee and weekend markets. El Tunco and El Zonte are the go-to surf beaches on the coast. For nature lovers, Parque Nacional El Imposible and the crater lakes of Santa Ana volcano are unmissable.

[Photo]

The Route

Most travellers fly into San Salvador (SAL) and use the capital as a brief launching pad before heading out to the more interesting parts of the country. I'd recommend spending no more than a night or two in the city — it's worth seeing Mercado Central and the historic centre, but the real magic is elsewhere.

The route below works well as a loop: head west into the highlands and Ruta de las Flores first, then curve down to the coast, and finish in the north around Suchitoto before flying out. You can also run it in reverse.

San Salvador

Land at SAL, grab a taxi or Uber into the city (around $15–20 USD). Check into your hostel in the Zona Rosa or San Benito neighbourhood — both are safe, walkable, and well-stocked with places to eat. Walk down to Plaza Barrios and the neoclassical Catedral Metropolitana. Eat pupusas for dinner.

[Photo]

Santa Ana

Head west to Santa Ana (about 1.5 hours, ~$1.50). The city has a beautiful neo-Gothic cathedral and a lively central market. From here, hike to the summit of Santa Ana Volcano (Ilamatepec) — one of the best volcano hikes in Central America. Guides depart from the park entrance at 11am. Also worth a visit: Lago Coatepeque, a stunning turquoise crater lake nearby.

[Photo]

Ruta de las Flores

This scenic mountain route connects a string of small towns — Nahuizalco, Salcoatitán, Juayúa, Apaneca, and Ataco — each with its own character. Juayúa hosts a famous food festival every weekend. Ataco has the most vibrant murals and craft shops. Spend two nights to do it properly.

[Photo]

El Tunco & The Coast

Head south to the coast. El Tunco is the backpacker surf hub: black sand beach, consistent waves, cheap beer, and a relaxed vibe. If you don't surf, El Zonte (a 10-minute ride) is calmer and has a beautiful stretch of beach. Sunsets here are extraordinary.

[Photo]

Suchitoto

El Salvador's most beautifully preserved colonial town — cobblestone streets, a 19th-century church, and views over Lago Suchitlán. Walk down to the lake for a sunset boat ride if time allows. From here, it's a 45-minute drive back to San Salvador.

[Photo]

1-Week Itinerary

1
Arrive San Salvador — settle in, explore the centre
Land at SAL, grab a taxi or Uber into the city (around $15–20 USD). Check into your hostel in the Zona Rosa or San Benito neighbourhood. In the afternoon, walk down to Plaza Barrios and the neoclassical Catedral Metropolitana. Eat pupusas for dinner. This is non-negotiable.
2
Santa Ana & Cerro Verde National Park
Take an early bus west to Santa Ana (about 1.5 hours, ~$1.50). The city has a beautiful neo-Gothic cathedral and a lively central market. In the afternoon, head up to Cerro Verde to hike around the rim of Lago Coatepeque — a stunning turquoise crater lake.
3
Hike Santa Ana Volcano
The hike to the summit of Santa Ana (Ilamatepec) is one of the best volcano hikes in Central America. About 2 hours up through cloud forest to the rim of a sulphurous crater lake. Guides are mandatory and depart from the park entrance at 11am. Wear layers — it gets cold near the top.
4–5
Ruta de las Flores
This scenic mountain route connects a string of small towns — Nahuizalco, Salcoatitán, Juayúa, Apaneca, and Ataco — each with its own character. Juayúa hosts a famous food festival every weekend. Ataco has the most vibrant murals and craft shops. Stay two nights to do it properly.
6
El Tunco — Pacific Coast
Head south to the coast. El Tunco is the backpacker surf hub: black sand beach, consistent waves, cheap beer, and a relaxed vibe. If you don't surf, El Zonte (a 10-minute ride) is calmer. Sunsets here are extraordinary.
7
Suchitoto — then fly home
Make your way north to Suchitoto, El Salvador's most beautifully preserved colonial town. Cobblestone streets, a 19th-century church, and views over Lago Suchitlán. Walk down to the lake for a sunset boat ride if time allows. From here, it's a 45-minute drive back to San Salvador for your flight.

2-Week Itinerary

Got two weeks? Add two or three nights in Suchitoto, a day trip to the Cinquera cloud forest reserve, and an extra couple of days on the coast. You could also cross into Honduras for the Copán ruins and loop back.

Getting Around

El Salvador is small enough to cover most of the country in a single day's drive. Chicken buses (repurposed American school buses) are the cheapest way to get between towns — $0.50–2 per ride — and are a cultural experience in themselves. For more comfort and flexibility, private shuttles (bookable through most hostels) run $10–20 per person and are worth it if you value time. Renting a car is a great option if you want to explore off the beaten track — roads are generally decent and GPS works well. Uber operates in and around San Salvador.

How Much Does It Cost

El Salvador is one of the more affordable destinations in Central America. Budget travellers can get by on $30–45 USD per day covering a hostel dorm, local food, and buses. Mid-range travellers staying in guesthouses and eating at restaurants should budget $60–90 per day. El Salvador uses the US dollar as its official currency (alongside Bitcoin, which became legal tender in 2021), so there's no need to worry about exchange rates. Pupusas — the national dish — cost less than $1 each and are absolutely worth eating at every opportunity.

Top Things to Do

Surf the black sand breaks at El Tunco, hike to the crater lake of Santa Ana volcano, and wander the indigo-painted streets of Suchitoto. Take a boat out onto Lago Suchitlán at sunset, explore the Joya de Cerén archaeological site (a Pompeii-like Maya village preserved under volcanic ash), and pick up handmade crafts along the Ruta de las Flores. Don't miss a morning at a local coffee farm in the highlands — El Salvador produces some excellent single-origin beans that rarely make it to export.

[Photo]

Safety

El Salvador has undergone a dramatic transformation in recent years. Following a major government crackdown on gang activity beginning in 2022, crime rates have fallen sharply and the country now feels notably safer than it did even a few years ago. Tourist areas, beaches, and colonial towns are generally relaxed and welcoming. That said, it's still worth exercising common sense — avoid displaying expensive gear, stick to well-travelled routes after dark, and check current travel advisories before your trip. Most travellers report feeling comfortable and safe throughout their visit.

Basic Facts

A quick reference for the practical things you will want to know before you go.

  • Visa: Citizens of the US, EU, UK, Canada, and Australia can enter visa-free for up to 90 days. El Salvador is part of the CA-4 agreement, meaning you share a 90-day allowance across Guatemala, Honduras, and Nicaragua.
  • Language: Spanish is the official language. English is spoken in tourist areas and surf towns.
  • Currency: US Dollar (USD). Bitcoin is also legal tender since 2021. ATMs are widely available in cities and larger towns.
  • Capital: San Salvador
  • Time Zone: UTC-6 (Central Standard Time, no daylight saving)
  • Electricity: 120V / 60Hz, Type A & B plugs (same as the US).

Practical Tips & Budget

Where to Stay

Hostels in El Salvador are excellent value — expect to pay $10–18 for a dorm bed, $30–50 for a private room. In Ataco, splurge on one of the boutique guesthouses if your budget allows. On the coast, most accommodation is open-air and casual.

Budget

I comfortably travelled El Salvador on $40–50 USD per day including accommodation, food, transport, and activities. The country uses US dollars, so there's no currency headache. Tipping is appreciated but not obligatory — rounding up the bill is always welcome.

Safety

El Salvador is genuinely safe for travellers right now following the dramatic reduction in gang crime since 2022. I never felt unsafe anywhere on this route. Use common sense, avoid flashing expensive gear, and you'll be absolutely fine.
