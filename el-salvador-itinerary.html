1–2 Weeks In El Salvador: A Complete Backpacking Itinerary

March 2026 · 12 min read · El Salvador

El Salvador is one of Central America's best kept secrets. It's tiny — you can drive coast to coast in under three hours — but it packs in volcanoes, surf beaches, colonial towns, and cloud forest all within reach of each other. Here's how I'd spend one to two weeks if I were doing it again.

Before you go: El Salvador is part of the CA-4 agreement. Your 90-day visa-free allowance is shared across Guatemala, Honduras, and Nicaragua — so if you're combining countries, keep track of your days.

The Route

Most travellers fly into San Salvador (SAL) and use the capital as a brief launching pad before heading out to the more interesting parts of the country. I'd recommend spending no more than a night or two in the city — it's worth seeing Mercado Central and the historic centre, but the real magic is elsewhere.

The route below works well as a loop: head west into the highlands and Ruta de las Flores first, then curve down to the coast, and finish in the north around Suchitoto before flying out. You can also run it in reverse.

One Week Itinerary

1
Arrive San Salvador — settle in, explore the centre
Land at SAL, grab a taxi or Uber into the city (around $15–20 USD). Check into your hostel in the Zona Rosa or San Benito neighbourhood — both are safe, walkable, and well-stocked with places to eat. In the afternoon, walk down to Plaza Barrios and the neoclassical Catedral Metropolitana. Eat pupusas for dinner. This is non-negotiable.
2
Santa Ana & Cerro Verde National Park
Take an early bus west to Santa Ana (about 1.5 hours, ~$1.50). The city has a beautiful neo-Gothic cathedral and a lively central market. In the afternoon, head up to Cerro Verde to hike around the rim of Lago Coatepeque — a stunning turquoise crater lake. Budget option: there are a few guesthouses on the lake's edge for a very reasonable price.
3
Hike Santa Ana Volcano
The hike to the summit of Santa Ana (Ilamatepec) is one of the best volcano hikes in Central America. It's about 2 hours up through cloud forest to the rim of a sulphurous crater lake. Guides are mandatory and depart from the park entrance at 11am. Wear layers — it gets cold near the top. Back in town, treat yourself to a cold Pilsener and a plate of yuca frita.
4–5
Ruta de las Flores
This scenic mountain route connects a string of small towns — Nahuizalco, Salcoatitán, Juayúa, Apaneca, and Ataco — each with its own character. Juayúa hosts a famous food festival every weekend. Ataco has the most Instagram-worthy murals and craft shops. Hire a tuk-tuk between towns or take the local buses. Stay two nights to do it properly — there are some excellent boutique guesthouses in Ataco.
6
El Tunco — Pacific Coast
Head south to the coast. El Tunco is the backpacker surf hub: black sand beach, consistent waves, cheap beer, and a relaxed vibe. It gets lively at night but is perfectly quiet during the day. If you don't surf, El Zonte (a 10-minute ride) is calmer and has a beautiful stretch of beach. Stay at least one night — sunsets here are extraordinary.
7
Suchitoto — then fly home
Make your way north to Suchitoto, El Salvador's most beautifully preserved colonial town. Blue and white cobblestone streets, a 19th-century church, and views over Lago Suchitlán. Walk down to the lake for a sunset boat ride if time allows. From here, it's a 45-minute drive back to San Salvador for your flight.

Got two weeks? Add two or three nights in Suchitoto, a day trip to the Cinquera cloud forest reserve, and an extra couple of days on the coast. You could also cross into Honduras for the Copán ruins and loop back.

Practical Notes

Getting around

Chicken buses between major towns cost $0.50–$2. Private shuttles (bookable through most hostels) run $10–20 per person between popular destinations and are worth it if you value time. Uber works well in and around San Salvador.

Where to stay

Hostels in El Salvador are excellent value — expect to pay $10–18 for a dorm bed, $30–50 for a private room. In Ataco, splurge on one of the boutique guesthouses if your budget allows; they're worth every dollar. On the coast, most accommodation is open-air and casual.

Budget

I comfortably travelled El Salvador on $40–50 USD per day including accommodation, food, transport, and activities. The country uses US dollars, so there's no currency headache. Tipping is appreciated but not obligatory — rounding up the bill is always welcome.

Safety

El Salvador is genuinely safe for travellers right now following the dramatic reduction in gang crime since 2022. I never felt unsafe anywhere on this route. Use common sense, avoid flashing expensive gear, and you'll be absolutely fine.
