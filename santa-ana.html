The Ultimate Guide to Santa Ana, El Salvador's Second City

Volcanoes, crater lakes, Mayan ruins, and the best pupusas you'll ever eat — Santa Ana delivers on every front.

Mar 22, 2026

Getting to Santa Ana

I started my journey by flying in to the airport (SAL) in San Salvador. It is located about 45 minutes outside of the capital city itself. After taking a red-eye flight, I was so exhausted and headed directly to Santa Ana, the second largest city in the country. You can take a bus (with a transfer in San Salvador) or an Uber directly to Santa Ana. Santa Ana is also connected by tourist shuttles to Guatemala (Lake Atitlan & El Paredon) so it is a popular first-stop for many backpackers.

The Many Day Trips of Santa Ana

The main draw of coming to Santa Ana is that a great base for day trips in the surrounding area. Here are some of the top things to do:

Santa Ana Volcano (Ilamatepec)

The main reason why people come to Santa Ana is the volcano hike. With a bright turquoise volcanic lake at the top, it is a unique volcano experience that is hard to find elsewhere. The hike itself is not particularly challenging and takes approximately ~2hrs each way (4.4 mi / 7.1 km). Although Santa Ana is an active volcano, it is generally considered safe since it hasn't erupted since 2005 and authorities are constantly monitoring the volcano.

There are several ways to do it:

  • Book a tour — this is the easiest way to do the Santa Ana Volcano hike and usually comes bundled with a trip to Lago de Coatepeque (more on that later). I spent $20 for the tour and it was well worth it not having to worry about logistics. I'm sure there were more luxury tour options, but riding in the back of a pickup truck felt like a quintessential El Salvador experience. There are also tour options with pickup from other cities in El Salvador like San Salvador and El Tunco if you're not based in Santa Ana (link here)
  • DIY — the volcano is less than an hour outside of Santa Ana so you can easily access the volcano by car, public transit, uber or taxi. However, for safety reasons, the government of El Salvador requires you to take a guide while hiking up the volcano. It costs only $4 per person, but only at specific times during the day. Here is a link to a detailed guide with more logistical details on planning the hiking on your own.

Lago de Coatepeque

This is a deep blue crater lake, that lies only 45 minutes from the city center of Santa Ana. It is accessible by bus (take the #242 / #220 bus from Terminal Francisco Lara Pineda). The lake offers watersports like boats, jet skis and kayaks for rental.

My tour included a stop at Captain Morgan Hostel. The hostel had a restaurant with bar, complete with a deck to jump off of. It was a relaxing place to swim around and spend an afternoon. Captain Morgan Hostel is also a popular spot for travelers who want to spend a day to relax by the lake.

El Salto de Malacatiupán

Hotsprings + waterfalls, ¿por que no los dos? This is a truly unique experience that you only can find in El Salvador. Located between about an hour from Santa Ana (also an hour from Juayua), the El Salto de Malacatiupán waterfalls are basically nature's jacuzzi flowing through a stunning waterfall. They are very easily accessible by bus, car, uber or moped. However, they also remain undiscovered by most tourists as there are no tours leading there.

The entrance fee was $1 that you pay at a restaurant situated at the entrance. There is a short path that splits one side leading down to the entrance of the waterfall and one side the river that you can bathe in. The water is true jacuzzi temperature (37°C or 100°F)! There were not many other tourists when I was there, just some locals washing clothes down the river (which gave it a really relaxing feel). The whole trip can be done in a half day including transport.

Parque Arqueológico Tazumal

Located in the neighboring town only 30 minutes outside of Santa Ana, lies the ancient Mayan city of Chalchuapa. First inhabited by the Mayans in 250AD, it was later inhabited by the Toltec population until 1,200AD. Today, it stands as the largest set of Mayan Ruins in El Salvador. The complex is relatively large with a large pyramid in the center (and you might even spot some iguanas running around!)

You can easily access this Mayan archaeological site by taking the 202 bus out of Santa Ana or a ~$7 uber. The entrance fee was only a few dollars. There are tours offered in both Spanish and English for those who want more historical context and a small museum with some historical artifacts. The site is easily visited in an hour or so and Casa Blanca, another archaeological site, lies within a short walk from Tazumal.

Santa Ana — The City Itself

The city itself isn't very touristy and can easily be explored in 1 day. Here are the top things to do in the city:

Parque Libertad

The main square in Santa Ana which is home to many of the historic buildings and regional government buildings. Most of the main tourists sights in the city are centered around here.

  • Catedral de Nuestra Señora Santa Ana (Santa Ana Cathedral) — a beautiful white, neo-gothic cathedral built originally in 1575. It has a very intricate facade and is the most impressive piece of architecture that I saw in El Salvador. It remains an important centerpiece for the city with locals taking photos there and young kids playing with the pigeons
  • National Theater of Santa Ana — a historic theater built in 1910 lies only a few hundred meters from the main cathedral. The theatre is used to host national cultural events, is very well maintained and you can take a tour inside for $5
  • Alcaldía Municipal de Santa Ana and Monumento a la Libertad — Two beautiful government buildings around the square

Hector's Walking Tour

One of my favorite things to do in El Salvador was Hector's Walking Tour. Given all the recent changes in El Salvador's history, I was very interested in learning first hand from a local how the country had changed and what was life like before Bukele. Hector was the perfect guide for that. He led a very open ended tour (every day is different, depending on what the group wants to do).

We started at a popular coffee shop called Beats Coffee Centro Historico where we got to have El Salvador's best coffee. He honestly answered any and every question that our group had about El Salvador. It really opened my eyes to how rough El Salvador's past was with the Civil War in the 80s and gang violence. He described to us how El Salvadorans have adapted to the newfound feeling of safety and the cautiousness he has toward current politics and the future. Feeling a bit hungry around lunchtime, he led us to the local open air market where we sampled local dishes and fruits. The tour is completely tip based and is different every day. Link here.

Pupusa Making Class at Casa Coco

I signed up for the Pupusa Making class at my hostel and it was so good that I did it twice! Led by the lovely owner of the hostel named Yolanda, it was a few hours of making Pupusas from scratch. If you don't know, Pupusas are the national dish of El Salvador which are basically a really thick flour tortillas stuffed with any combination of stuffings. The most popular varieties are Revueltas meaning mixed with refried beans, Chicharrón and cheese as well as Loroco, a local herbal flower bud mixed with cheese.

The class is offered most days of the week (link here), lasts a few hours in the evening and costs $15. It was a really fun time complete with music and dancing. As a group of 8, we ended up making over 75 pupusas. Yolanda took me and one other traveler to give out our extras to the homeless.

Museo Regional de Occidente (Western Regional Museum)

The former mint for the El Salvadoran Colón before they adopted the U.S. Dollar as their main currency in 2001. It has now been transformed into the main historical museum in Santa Ana. I can't personally recommend the museum for everyone. There were some ancient artifacts and it explained the history of the Santa Ana region. The exhibits seemed a bit underfunded and the exhibits were all in Spanish. For only $3 entrance, I can't complain too much.

Old Art School

Located a bit further south outside of downtown Santa Ana, there are the ruins of an Old Art School. Once the vibrant center of creativity for Santa Ana, the Mariano Mendez Art School was abandoned. Popular with photographers, the art school serves as a reminder of Santa Ana's rich cultural heritage. Unfortunately, I showed up without a permit so I couldn't explore the site itself. A permit can be easily acquired from the local tourist office, but requires some advance planning.

Where to Eat!

Breakfast, lunch and dinner — El Salvadorans love their pupusas! You can find them on almost every street corner and I was never disappointed. However, there are many other dishes to try. Here were some of my favorite places to eat in Santa Ana:

  • Pupusería La Ceiba — considered the best pupuseria in Santa Ana!
  • Comedor y Pupuseria Claudia — a local spot offering a variety of dishes and pupusas
  • Azados — BBQ every Sunday — it may not look like much on google maps, but this was one of my favorite meals in El Salvador. Every Sunday, this family has a bbq and a large plate is only $5
  • Beats Coffee Centro Historico — home to an nationally recognized barista, this is a great place to sample some of El Salvador's best coffee
  • Karlita Café — a bakery / cafe which served a variety of interesting breakfast options
  • Street Stands around Parque Libertad — very cheap option (if you're feeling a bit adventurous!)

Places to Stay

There are many wonderful places to stay in Santa Ana ranging from hostels to hotels to luxury hotels. Note that many places in El Salvador only offer cold water showers so be prepared!

Hostels

Casa Coco — I stayed here personally and had a wonderful time. Casa Coco is a small hostel with a strong communal vibe. The owner, Yolanda, was very sweet and welcoming. The hostel offered a lot of tours (Santa Ana Volcano and 7 waterfalls) and activities (pupusa making class) all for a fair price. I had a wonderful time here.

Rick's Hostel — They have two locations: 1 in Santa Ana and 1 in Juayua — Ruta de Las Flores. The hostel offers motorbike rentals for $15 a day. They also have a service where they will transport your bags from one location to another if you want to make the journey between the two cities by moped. I ran into the owner (not named Rick!) a couple times on my trip, but a very friendly guy. rickshostels.com
