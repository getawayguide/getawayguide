Field Notes

Armenia

Yerevan  ·  Lake Sevan  ·  Khor Virap  ·  Geghard

General

  • Super underrated destination — cleaner and more modern than you'd expect, especially Yerevan
  • Get the GG app (Armenian Uber) and Yandex for getting around. Both work great
  • People speak a lot of Russian as a second language, even greeting you with "zdravice" — knowing a few words of Russian goes a long way
  • Cash is king outside of Yerevan. Withdraw AMD before heading anywhere rural
  • Renting a car for a day is 100% worth it to hit the stuff outside Yerevan — roads are beautiful and easy to navigate

Yerevan

3+ days recommended
  • The Cascade — massive Soviet-era staircase structure with modern art sculptures scattered throughout. The real highlight is the view of Mt. Ararat from the top. Snow-capped peaks looming over the city is genuinely magical
  • Republic Square — the main square with beautiful pink/red sandstone Soviet buildings and a nightly fountain light show at 8pm. They play classical music and shoot water like fireworks for 30 minutes. Grab some wine and watch
  • Free walking tours — highly recommend doing one. Super informative on Armenian history, Soviet legacy, the genocide, and the geopolitical situation. Small groups and very candid
  • Armenian Genocide Museum — sobering but really important. The brutalist cone memorial with the eternal flame is striking. Give it a few hours
  • Vernissage Market — outdoor souvenir market, better on weekends when it's fully open. Look for the hanging walnut candy (Soujuk)
  • GUM Market — the main indoor market. Great for dried fruits, cognac, and a rock-hard dried meat sausage called Sujuk
  • Mother Armenia — huge bronze statue up in Victory Park, surrounded by Soviet military equipment (tanks, jets, missiles). Very cool to wander around
  • Coffee culture is huge — Armenians use coffee the way Italians do. There are also instant coffee machines literally everywhere on the street
Where to eat
  • Anteb Western Armenian Cuisine — get the Lahmajun and Zaatar bread. Western Armenian food is similar to Turkish food since that region is now in Turkey
  • Tumanyan Khinkhali — good spot for large Pelmeni dumplings. Fancier vibe
  • Mayrig Yerevan — the fanciest place I went. The Armenian Manti here is baked (not boiled like Turkish) and served with tomato sauce and sour cream. Really unique
  • Yerevan Tavern — get the Nraneh (lamb with pomegranate juice) or the lamb liver and heart dish if you're feeling adventurous

Outside Yerevan

Day trip by rental car
  • Lake Sevan — the most famous lake in Armenia, stunning blue water with mountains all around. Stop at Sevanavank Monastery on the hill for the view. Random old guy will try to put a pigeon on your shoulder
  • Orgov Space Telescope — this was my favorite thing in Armenia. An abandoned Soviet-era radio telescope complex in the middle of nowhere. Incredible control room with pastel murals and Russian dials, plus a massive satellite dish to climb around. Technically need a government permit but you can pay the guys at the gate ~2,000 AMD each. Basically no other tourists. Do not skip this
  • Black Wall — a hidden abandoned mine site under grassy hills. No signs, no tourists. Cool to stumble upon
  • Khor Virap Monastery — one of the oldest monasteries in the world with Mt. Ararat directly behind it. The contrast of the black interior church with a beam of light cutting through is really beautiful
  • Geghard Monastery — half building, half cave carved into a hillside. Considered one of the most sacred spots in Armenia. Ancient and genuinely impressive
  • Garni Temple — pagan temple from 77AD that looks like a mini Acropolis perched on a cliff. Somehow survived the conversion to Christianity because a church was built right next to it
  • Symphony of Stones — natural hexagonal basalt columns formed by cooling lava, about 5-6 stories tall. Hidden in a gorgeous green valley with a river running through it
