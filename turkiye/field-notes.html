Field Notes

Turkiye

Istanbul  ·  Cappadocia  ·  Antalya  ·  Kaş  ·  Ölüdeniz

General

  • Yandex works for taxis in Turkey too. Taxis otherwise can try to overcharge you
  • Turkish breakfast (simit, eggs, cheese, olives, tomatoes) is amazing everywhere and usually cheap
  • Çay (small black tea) is the social drink. It's everywhere, costs almost nothing, and is delicious — put one sugar cube in
  • The Lira situation is real — the largest bill is 200 TL (~$4) so you end up with comical amounts of cash. Plan accordingly at ATMs
  • Turkey was noticeably less busy than usual in May due to regional instability — could be worth checking current conditions before you go

Istanbul

3+ days recommended
  • Give it at least 3 days — it's one of the great cities in the world and rewards slow exploration. The European and Asian sides each have a completely different vibe
  • Hagia Sophia — originally a Byzantine cathedral, then a mosque, then a museum, now a mosque again. One of the most impressive buildings ever built. The scale of the dome is hard to comprehend in person. Go early to avoid crowds and bring something to cover your shoulders/head
  • Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque) — right across from Hagia Sophia. Six minarets, stunning blue Iznik tile interior. Still an active mosque so it closes during prayer times — plan around that
  • Basilica Cistern — underground Byzantine cistern beneath the city. Atmospheric, dimly lit and genuinely impressive. The Medusa head columns are the highlight. Worth the entrance fee
  • Topkapi Palace — the primary residence of Ottoman sultans for 400 years. Enormous complex with multiple courtyards, treasury, harem and incredible Bosphorus views. Budget at least half a day. The treasury with the Spoonmaker's Diamond and Topkapi Dagger is the standout
  • Grand Bazaar — one of the oldest and largest covered markets in the world. 4,000+ shops selling everything from spices to gold to leather. Go to browse and absorb the atmosphere rather than to buy — prices are heavily inflated for tourists. Fun to get lost in
  • Nuruosmaniye Mosque — stunning baroque Ottoman mosque right next to the Grand Bazaar. Often overlooked compared to the big two but really beautiful inside
  • Balat — former Jewish neighborhood on the European side with colorful painted houses on steep cobblestone streets. One of the most photogenic neighborhoods in the city. Lots of good cafes and antique shops
  • Galata Tower — medieval stone tower in the Karaköy neighborhood. The view from the top is great but the real draw is the neighborhood below — tons of good coffee shops, restaurants and street life
  • Karaköy neighborhood — the trendiest area in Istanbul right now. Great coffee shops, street food and a mix of old Ottoman buildings and new design studios. Walk here from the Galata Tower
  • Fish sandwiches by the Galata Bridge — iconic Istanbul street food. Boats moored along the Golden Horn grill fresh mackerel right on deck and serve them in bread. Touristy but genuinely delicious and part of the experience
  • Bosphorus — the strait dividing Europe and Asia is worth a ferry ride at minimum. Cheap public ferries go between the European and Asian sides constantly. The Asian side (Kadıköy neighborhood) is less touristy and worth an afternoon if you have time
Where to eat
  • Doner is everywhere and ranges from amazing to average — go where locals are eating, not where tour buses stop
  • Simit (sesame bread rings) from street carts for breakfast — about 5 lira each
  • Damak — reliable local doner spot, nothing fancy but good

Cappadocia

  • One of the most surreal landscapes in the world. Stay in Göreme — it's the main base and has the best access to everything
  • Stay in a cave hotel if you can — they're surprisingly affordable and the atmosphere is completely unique. Terra Cave Hotel in Göreme is a solid option
  • Hot air balloon ride — the single best thing to do in Cappadocia. Balloons launch at sunrise and float over the fairy chimneys and valleys. Book in advance (especially in high season) — reputable operators fill up weeks out. It's expensive (~$150-200+) but genuinely one of the most spectacular experiences you can have anywhere in the world. Even if you don't do the ride, watching the balloons from your hotel roof at sunrise is magical
  • Göreme Open Air Museum — a UNESCO site with rock-cut Byzantine churches decorated with stunning frescoes dating back to the 10th century. The frescoes in the Dark Church are the most impressive. An easy walk from Göreme town center
  • Love Valley — the valley with the famous tall phallic rock formations. Worth a hike through — the landscape is genuinely alien and the views from the ridge above are incredible
  • White Valley — quieter than Love Valley with beautiful white and cream colored rock formations. Great for hiking and photography
  • Red Valley — named for the reddish volcanic rock. Best at sunset when the colors go vivid orange and red. One of the most beautiful hikes in the region
  • Uçhisar Castle — the highest point in Cappadocia, a rock fortress you can climb for a panoramic view of the entire region. Worth the climb, especially if you can time it around sunset
  • The valleys are all connected and you can hike between them — Love Valley to White Valley to Red Valley makes a great half-day loop
Where to eat
  • My Mother's Restaurant in Göreme — get the Manti (Turkish dumplings with yogurt and chili butter). Simple, cheap and very good
  • Testi Kebab (pottery kebab) — the regional specialty. Meat and vegetables slow-cooked in a sealed clay pot that gets broken open at the table. Worth doing at least once

Antalya

  • Honestly pretty touristy and catered to British package tourists, but the old town (Kaleiçi) is really pretty
  • Kaleiçi Old Town — covered in tan marble with Hadrian's Gate at the entrance. Nice to wander for an afternoon
  • Lower Düden Falls — free, easy to get to by bus. A huge waterfall that drops directly into the ocean. Really cool scale when you see boats at the bottom dwarfed by the falls
  • Upper Düden Falls — more of a tourist park feel but worth it for the cave tunnels you can walk through around the falls and the bright turquoise river
  • Sagalassos Ancient City — a 3 hour drive from Antalya but absolutely worth it if you have a car. Massive ancient Roman city with a theatre, baths, main square, and a recently reconstructed fountain. 95% Turkish tourists so it feels very un-touristy. Stop for çay at the local spot on the way in
Where to eat
  • Paşa Bey — get the Iskander kebab (bread cooked in tomato sauce with cream). One of the best meals in Turkey
  • Tarihi Ankara Simit Fırını — local bakery, great for breakfast. Try the Menemen (Turkish scrambled eggs with tomato) or the meat and cheese börek

Köprülü Canyon Area

  • Cactus Rafting white water tour — only $30 all in and includes a jeep safari, zipline, canyon overlook, and rafting. The rapids are mild (level 2) but the scenery makes it worth it. Great day trip from Antalya

Kaş

  • Really cute small town with nice alleys, a marina, and an ancient amphitheater. Way more charming than Antalya
  • Antiphellos Ancient City Amphitheater — small but very well restored, easy walk from town
  • Worth stopping for coffee with a marina view and just wandering the old town streets

Ölüdeniz / Fethiye Area

  • Paragliding in Ölüdeniz — the best thing I did in Turkey. You launch from a platform at the top of a mountain and glide for about 30 minutes over the bright blue lagoon below. More fun than skydiving or bungee jumping in my opinion. The Blue Lagoon below is stunning
  • Lycian Way — long distance hiking trail between Fethiye and Ölüdeniz (~17 miles). Beautiful pine forest, Mediterranean brush, Lycian tombs along the cliffs, and ghost town ruins of an abandoned Greek Christian village called Karakoy that was emptied during the 1923 population exchange. Really haunting and impressive
  • Fethiye Old Town — nice for a wander. Good promenade along the water stretching 3+ miles
  • Ancient City of Telmessos — hand-carved temple and rock tombs embedded in the hillside above Fethiye. Quick visit but interesting
Where to eat
  • Lokum Kokoreç — try the Kokoreç (lamb intestine sandwich) if you're feeling brave. A Turkish street food specialty
  • Yeşil Asma Yaprağı — homestyle food served cafeteria-style. Get a tray and point. Really good value
