North Macedonia Travel Guide with Perfect 8 Day Itinerary
Everything you need to plan a trip through North Macedonia, from crystal clear lakes, a bizarre capital full of statues to idyllic mountain towns

When I told people I was going to North Macedonia, I could see the wheels spinning in the back of their head trying to figure out where it was on the map or why someone would even go there. I'll admit, it is a bit of an obscure travel destination and that's part of what makes it so great. North Macedonia really surprised me with the impressive range of sights from crystal clear lakes, a bizarre capital full of statues, Roman ruins to idyllic mountain towns and fascinating geopolitics.

A 4-story Alexander the Great in Skopje and a pirate ship in the middle of Skopje!

To get you oriented, North Macedonia is a small, landlocked country located in the Balkans and bordered by Greece, Albania, Kosovo, Serbia and Bulgaria. It is often overlooked in favor of more popular countries in the region. I came in with fairly low expectations, having already traveled extensively around the Balkans, and it ended up blowing me away.

North Macedonia is full of things you won't find anywhere else: a capital nicknamed the "Disneyland of the Balkans," Skopje, covered in modern bronze heroes and fake pirate ships, Ohrid, a historic town full of medieval churches on a lake so clear you can see schools of fish swimming from the dock, and the Makedonium, a Yugoslav monument in the mountains that looks like a spaceship. The country doesn't get nearly as many tourists as other European destinations, you'll have most sights all to yourself outside of Lake Ohrid.

This guide covers everything you need to know including the basics of visiting North Macedonia, plus an 8-day itinerary that covers the highlights of the country. North Macedonia is also an easy addition to a broader Balkans trip and is well connected to places like Greece, Albania, Kosovo and Bulgaria.

Why Visit North Macedonia?
Section 01
Why Visit North Macedonia?
01Why Visit North Macedonia?

North Macedonia flies under the radar, even compared to its neighbors. Most of the people who do come only spend a couple days seeing Lake Ohrid. So, is the rest of it worth the trip? I had already traveled extensively around the Balkans (visiting Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia, Serbia and Montenegro), and I still didn't know what to expect. By the end of the week it had become one of my favorite countries in the region. Here's why:

  • Nature — Lake Ohrid is one of the oldest lakes in the world (about 1.36 million years) and so clear you can see schools of fish swimming from the dock, and the mountains around Krushevo are covered in pine forest, with views across the whole valley from the top of town
  • Fascinating and absurd history — this is a country that you probably never think about, but the history is fascinating. First, the naming issue: Greece strongly believes that Macedonians are stealing Greek culture by naming their country "Macedonia". This was such a big issue that it imposed a trade embargo on their neighboring country, made the country change its official name and flag. They also blocked North Macedonia from joining NATO for over a decade until the country finally changed its name to North Macedonia in 2019. Meanwhile, the government covered Skopje in hundreds of statues and fake classical buildings, but more on that later...
  • Authenticity — in a world of curated Instagram travel, North Macedonia felt like an authentic travel experience, away from the crowds and influencer hype. You get to see the real side of the country and you'll often have the sights all to yourself: I shared the ruins of an ancient Roman city, Heraclea Lyncestis, with just 2 other people, and nobody else was at the Makedonium when I visited
  • Affordability — North Macedonia is a European destination that won't break the budget. In fact, it was one of the cheapest places I've traveled in Europe: an entire apartment for $30 to $50 a night, a dinner with a beer for under $10 and buses for $5 to $10 between most cities. Go now, before the rest of Europe catches on!

Top Things to Do
Section 02
Top Things to Do
02Top Things to Do

You can drive from one end of North Macedonia to the other in just under 4 hours, so it's easy to pack a lot in one trip. If I had to pick just three experiences, these are the ones I'd build a trip around:

  • Lake Ohrid — the reason most people come, and it absolutely lives up to the hype. The lake dates back 1.36 million years, the old town above it is packed with 11th- to 14th-century churches, and the spring at St. Naum is the clearest I've ever seen. Make sure to take a boat tour while you're there. It was the best day I had in the country
  • The Picturesque Mountain Town of Krushevo — the highest town in North Macedonia, with white houses and red roofs filling a pine valley and a 1974 Yugoslav monument shaped like a spaceship on the hill above. Hands down my favorite place in the whole country
  • The Statues of Skopje 2014 — I've talked a lot about this so far, only because it was so spectacularly odd. Spend a day in Skopje admiring the 4-story Alexander the Great statue, a bridge lined with bronze figures and 3 pirate ships parked in a river in a landlocked country. None of it makes sense, and I loved it
The spring water at St. Naum on the boat tour, and the Makedonium above Krushevo

Picking a top three wasn't easy. For the rest of my favorites, see my Top 10 Things to Do in North Macedonia article.

The Route: Suggested 8 Day Itinerary
Section 03
The Route: Suggested 8 Day Itinerary
03The Route: Suggested 8 Day Itinerary

With 8 days in North Macedonia, you can see all of the highlights without rushing: the statues of Skopje, a few days on Lake Ohrid, the mountain town of Krushevo and the Roman ruins outside Bitola. It is a perfect amount of time to get a sense of Macedonian culture and what makes the country so unique. I spent 8 days in North Macedonia at the end of May 2026, starting in Skopje, moving on to Ohrid and finishing up in the mountains at Krushevo and in Bitola before crossing into Greece. The route below follows the same 8 days, with the bus connections lined up for you.

No ride on this route is longer than about 4 hours, but plan your travel days around the timetables and check departures in person the day before. Every stop has its own dedicated guide so pick a destination if you're interested in reading more: Skopje, Ohrid, Krushevo and Bitola.

Suggested Route Overview
1
Land in Skopje and Walk the Statues of Skopje 2014
Day 1
Land in Skopje and Walk the Statues of Skopje 2014

Most flights land at Skopje International Airport, located 25 minutes east of the city. Skip the taxi and take the airport shuttle and lands right in the downtown at Macedonia Square for about 200 denars ($3.50). Stay in anywhere near the city center and all the major attractions will be walking distance. My personal favorite is Debar Maalo, a leafy neighborhood of cafes 10 minutes on foot from the square.

More bronze figures than pedestrians on the Bridge of Civilizations

Spend the afternoon admiring the statues of Skopje 2014 from the 4-story Alexander the Great Statue, the bronze figures along the Bridge of Civilizations and 3 pirate ships parked in the river. If you're looking for a challenge, try to count every single one of them. Finish in the Old Bazaar with kebap at Kaj Гоце, a tiny spot run by a friendly older Macedonian gentleman.

The Old Bazaar and a traditional Kebap shop
2
Skopje's History, Brutalism and Kale Fortress
Day 2
Skopje's History, Brutalist Architecture and Kale Fortress

Join Free Skopje Walking Tours at 10 AM on Macedonia Square. It's a free walking tour that lasts 2 to 3 hours and is tip-based, so you pay what you think it was worth. The tour covers all of downtown Skopje and includes a burek stop. You'll learn about the unique history of North Macedonia from a local ranging from the Yugoslavia Era, Skopje 2014 and the name dispute with Greece.

In the afternoon, go see the brutalist concrete side of the city. My favorites were the dystopian looking Central Post Office, a jarring concrete tower with purple windows and the round, black-arched Archbishop Cathedral of St. Clement of Ohrid, covered in gold murals inside.

On the way, stop at the Mother Teresa Memorial House, a small glass museum dedicated to Mother Teresa who was originally born in Skopje and the Church of St. Constantine and Helena right next door. Only finished in 2025, it has intricate geometric stonework on the outside and is painted in gold on blue from floor to ceiling. Then catch the sunset from Kale Fortress, free and 10 minutes above the bazaar, where you'll probably have the walls to yourself.

3–4
Ohrid's Old Town, St. John Kaneo and the Waterfront
Day 3–4
Ohrid's Old Town, St. John Kaneo and the Waterfront

Take a morning bus to Ohrid from the Skopje bus station. They leave about every hour and take 3 to 4 hours. In Ohrid, stay near the old town and everything will be walking distance. I personally recommend Old Town Hostel Ohrid which was hands down the best hostel in the country.

Spend the first evening by the water, walking from St. Clement of Ohrid Square along the promenade to the Old Swimming Court, a crumbling old dock with the best view back at the old town around sunset.

Historic Ohrid town on the shores of a pristine lake with incredibly clear water

On day 4, get to the iconic St. John Kaneo Church early, before the tour groups show up, and keep going a few minutes for a quiet getaway near Labino Vista Point. Then climb past Plaošnik to Samuel's Fortress, head down through the Upper Gate past the Ancient Macedonian Theatre of Lychnidos and finish at the Old Town View Point, the view I liked best on the hill. Spend the rest of the day getting lost in the side streets of the old town or relaxing at a nearby cafe.

St. John Kaneo overlooking Lake Ohrid and the view nearby at Labino Vista Point
5
A Full-Day Boat Tour to St. Naum
Day 5
A Full-Day Boat Tour to St. Naum Monastery

The following day will be spent enjoying the beauty and tranquility of Lake Ohrid. Book the full-day boat tour with Lale Tours, a family run operator from a small village on the east shore. The tour last about 8 hours including pickup, drinks and lunch, for $80 to $100. This ended up being my favorite day in my entire time in North Macedonia.

The tour starts with the Bay of Bones, a rebuilt Bronze Age village on stilts, and then we headed up to the beautiful viewpoint over Trpejca, the "Macedonian Saint Tropez." We then continued on to St. Naum Monastery, a 10th-century monastery near the Albanian border. Make sure you join the optional rowboat (20€ for three of us). The boat will take you to a tranquil spring channel fed by Lake Prespa, so clear that you can see the individual grains of sand on the bottom of the channel. The tour culminates in a barbecue lunch on the beach at St. Zaum.

St. Zaum, our lunch stop, and the water right off its beach
6–7
Up the Mountain to Krushevo
Day 6–7
Up the Mountain to Krushevo

The next stop takes you high into the mountains of North Macedonia to the highest town in the country, Krushevo. It will mostly be a travel day with a 2-leg bus journey: first from Ohrid to Prilep (about 2 hours), then a minibus for the last 30 to 60 minutes up to Krushevo, all for about $2. Be sure to check schedules in advance as online schedules are unreliable.

Krushevo is a small mountain town of just 4,000 people, with white houses and red roofs filling a pine valley. Walk up to the mysterious space age Makedonium monument and learn about the Krushevo Republic. Enjoy the views of the picturesque mountain town, eat sarma at Krushevska Odaja and spend the night at Hotel Montana Palace, a 4-star hotel with the best views overlooking the whole town.

On day 7, hike into the thick pine forests out to Mechkin Kamen. The hike is relatively easy and is about 5 miles (2 hours) round trip on a forest trail that starts behind the hotel. At the end, a bronze rebel holds up a rock, because that's what the fighters of the Krushevo Republic were left with once their ammunition ran out.

Spend the rest of the day wandering around town experiencing traditional Macedonian life and architecture, unchanged for centuries. You'll see tractors driving through the main roads, locals chopping firewood for winter and dozens of old Zastavas parked on the side of the road. You could see Krushevo in a day, but the second night is what lets you slow down to its pace.

8
Bitola and the Ruins of Heraclea Lyncestis
Day 8
Bitola and the Ruins of Heraclea Lyncestis

The final stop on our trip is the city of Bitola, located right on the Greek border. I only came here to cross into Greece, but it ended up being the biggest pleasant surprise of my trip. Stroll down the main walking street called Širok Sokak, lined with beautiful yellow consulate-era buildings, all the way to up to green Magnolia Square. Then cross into the old bazaar for a Turkish coffee with the locals.

Bitola's main walking street, and the snowy peaks of Pelister behind the bazaar

Later, walk 30 minutes through Bitola City Park to the ancient Roman ruins of Heraclea Lyncestis, where the floor is lined with well-preserved mosaics of peacocks and lions. The next day, ride an hourly bus back to Skopje (3 to 4 hours), or have your host book a transfer to Florina in Greece for about €40.

The full Heraclea Lyncestis complex with mosaics lining the floor

Your trip doesn't have to be limited to the places I saw. Travelers I met who had more time kept recommending these, and great places to spend a few extra days:

  • Tetovo — a city located 45-minute west of Skopje. The highlight is Painted Mosque with an extremely intricate hand-painted dome and considered one of the most beautiful buildings in the Balkans
  • Pelister National Park — the best national park in the country and a great place to spend a few days hiking in the mountains of Macedonia. The park is covered in forests of rare Macedonian pine and the classic hike to Golemo Ezero culminates in a glacial lake just below the summit
  • Kosovo — one of the most interesting and contentious countries in the Balkans. Spend 2 to 5 days learned about the unique geopolitical situations and make sure to visit the beautiful Ottoman old town of Prizren
Tetovo — a 45-minute bus ride west of Skopje for the Painted Mosque, covered in painted flowers instead of tiles. The day trip from Skopje I was most sorry to miss
Pelister National Park — the snowy peaks behind Bitola. The classic hike to Golemo Ezero, a glacial lake, takes 6 to 7 hours round trip
Kosovo — Pristina is only 2 hours from Skopje, but give the country 2 to 3 days so you have time for the Ottoman old town of Prizren too

Macedonian Food
Section 04
Macedonian Food
04Macedonian Food

Macedonian food is hearty Balkan cooking built around grilled meat, fresh bread, peppers and cheese, with a strong influence from its neighbors and from 500 years of Ottoman rule. Peppers are the backbone of it all: most meals come with a bowl of ajvar, the smoky roasted red pepper spread that families still make by the crate every fall. Best of all, it's cheap: a sit-down lunch or dinner rarely costs more than $10. These are the dishes to look for:

  • Shopska salad — chopped tomatoes, cucumbers, onions and peppers under a snowy pile of grated white cheese. It starts almost every meal, and on a hot day it's all you need
  • Tavče gravče — the national dish: white beans slow-baked in a clay pan with onions, dried red peppers and paprika until the top turns crisp and the inside is thick and smoky. It's simple comfort food that comes to the table still bubbling in the pan. Try it at Restaurant Skopski Merak in Skopje
  • Kebap — small rolls of minced beef and lamb grilled over charcoal (what Bosnians call ćevapi), served by the half dozen or dozen with warm flatbread and a pile of raw onion. You can find it all over the country, but my favorite spot was Kaj Гоце in Skopje's Old Bazaar
Kebap with onions and fries at Kaj Гоце
  • Burek — flaky layers of thin pastry filled with cheese, meat or spinach. It's a must anytime I'm in this part of the world. Perfect for breakfast or a quick snack and about $2 at any bakery
  • Ayran — a cold, salty yogurt drink and burek's natural partner. It is an acquired taste and might sound strange at first, but it's the perfect match for any pastry
  • Polneti piperki and Sarma — peppers or cabbage leaves stuffed with minced meat and rice, topped with a big spoonful of sour cream
Polneti piperki with a scoop of sour cream at Viva Ksantika kaj Tanja
  • Pljeskavica — a Balkan burger patty infused with spices, best with kajmak (a rich, creamy cheese) melted in. It's grilled to order and stuffed into a fluffy flatbread with onions and ajvar, a cheap and filling meal on the go
  • Selsko meso — chicken and pork meatballs stewed with tomato and cheese in a clay pot. Marchelo Lounge Bar in Krushevo is the only place I saw it
  • Turkish coffee — the most popular way to drink coffee in the country, and a remnant of more than 500 years of Ottoman rule. It's brewed in a small long-handled copper pot and served thick and strong in a tiny cup, with the grounds settled at the bottom (skip the last sip) and often a cube of Turkish delight on the side. When to Visit
Section 04
When to Visit
04When to Visit

The best time to visit North Macedonia is May and June, or September and October. I visited at the end of May: the mountains around Krushevo were green, the lake was calm and quiet and prices hadn't climbed for summer yet.

July and August bring real heat inland, with Skopje often topping 95°F (35°C), and that's when Ohrid fills up with visitors from the neighboring countries. Winters are cold, with Skopje hovering around freezing (32°F, 0°C) from December to February and snow covers the mountains. That's when Krushevo turns into a little ski town with the Stanič ski center on its doorstep. Unless you're coming to ski, it's the hardest season for a trip: the days are short and the lake towns go quiet.

Getting There
Section 05
Getting There
05Getting There

If you're traveling internationally, you'll likely fly into Skopje International Airport (SKP). The airport is a hub for the budget airline Wizz Air, with direct flights from more than 30 cities across Europe, and there are connections through Istanbul or Vienna from farther away. You can also fly into Ohrid which has a small airport with seasonal flights or fly into Tirana, Albania and take a 3 hour bus into the country.

North Macedonia is also an easy addition to a longer trip across the Balkans. Traveling by bus around the region is extremely cheap: Skopje has direct buses from every neighboring country, and most tickets cost $10 to $30. Gjirafa Travel and FlixBus list nearly every route, so check both to compare departure times and prices before you book.

Here are the direct buses into Skopje from each neighboring country:

  • Kosovo — about 9 buses a day from Pristina (about 2 hours), €8.50 to €10 ($10 to $11)
  • Bulgaria — 2 to 3 buses a day from Sofia (4.5 to 5 hours), from about €27 ($30)
  • Albania — 5 to 6 buses a day from Tirana (5 to 6 hours with the border), from about €21 ($23)
  • Serbia — at least 6 buses a day from Belgrade (about 6 hours), from about €26 ($29)
  • Montenegro — 1 or 2 buses a day from Podgorica (9 to 10 hours), from about €24 ($26)
  • Greece — the tricky border. Only 1 or 2 direct buses a day leave Thessaloniki (4 to 4.5 hours, from about €36 or $40), so most people cross between Bitola and Florina by taxi instead
By Car or Bus
Section 06
By Car or Bus
06By Car or Bus
On board one of the minibuses that connect the smaller towns

Once you're in the country, getting around North Macedonia is cheap and fairly easy. You can drive across the entire country in around 4 hours and there is an extensive network of buses linking every city. Most rides will only cost $5 to $10. Within each town, you'll walk almost everywhere.

The one catch is the timetables. Schedules for the major cities are reliable, but once you start to venture off to the smaller towns (like Krushevo), the schedules become more sparse. I used the website Balkan Viator as my main source for bus times. However, it is often out of date, so double-check at the station the day before. Additionally, almost everything is closed on Sundays so plan accordingly.

  • Intercity buses — about every hour between Skopje, Ohrid and Bitola, and the easiest way to follow this route. Buy your ticket at the station, or online with Transkop for Bitola
  • Rental car — rent a car from Skopje Airport for just $25 to $35 a day. It provides the most flexibility, especially when traveling to smaller towns. Once you're out of Skopje, traffic is light and the driving is pretty relaxed
  • Taxis and ridesharing apps — there's no Uber or Bolt in the country, and the local apps (Wizi and BeeRide in Skopje) are hit or miss. Regular taxis are cheap, but just make sure to agree a price before you getting in

Budget Breakdown
Section 07
Budget Breakdown
07Budget Breakdown

North Macedonia was the cheapest country I've ever visited in Europe. A whole Airbnb apartment ran $30 to $50 a night. Meals were affordable ranging from a $2 burek for breakfast to a $10 sit-down dinner with a beer. Entry to the big sights is extremely cheap: 120 denars ($2) for Samuel's Fortress and 180 denars ($3) for Heraclea Lyncestis.

Backpackers can get by on $25 to $50 a day for a hostel bed, bakery breakfasts and buses. With $55 to $110 a day, you'll have your own apartment and can eat wherever you like. The two splurges worth saving for are the full-day boat tour on Lake Ohrid ($80 to $100) and a night at Hotel Montana Palace in Krushevo ($85) for the view off the balcony.

Safety
Section 08
Safety
08Safety

North Macedonia is a very safe country with limited petty crime. People often assume the Balkans are a dangerous place associating it with organized crime and the wars in the region during the 90s. However if you believe the US travel advisory website, it is has a level 1 designation, considered even safer than popular Western European destinations like France, Italy and Spain. I always felt safe during my time there and I walked around Skopje, Ohrid and Bitola late at night without a second thought.

Since it isn't a popular tourist destination, North Macedonians were happy to welcome you to their country. People also always went out of their way to help even when it is out of their way. You'll definitely experience the famous "Balkan hospitality" when you're there.

Visa Requirements
Section 09
Visa Requirements
09Visa Requirements

If you have a U.S., Canadian, EU, U.K. or Australian passport, you can stay in North Macedonia for up to 90 days without a visa. There's no fee, just a stamp.

Immigration at the Skopje airport took me only a couple of minutes. Crossing overland is just as easy: leaving for Greece at Medžitlija, the only extra step was having my fingerprints taken.

Currency
Section 10
Currency
10Currency

The official currency is the Macedonian denar (MKD). Cards are fine at hotels and bigger restaurants in Skopje and Ohrid, but bakeries, buses, taxis and most of Krushevo and Bitola run on cash. Some larger prices were quoted in euros instead: my rowboat at St. Naum, the taxi up to Krushevo and my transfer to Greece were all paid that way.

Carry small bills, since ATMs hand out big notes nobody can break. My taxi driver to Krushevo and I had to circle the town twice to find change.

1 USD ≈ 54.87 MKD (mid-market, Nov 11, 2026).
Language
Section 11
Language
11Language

Macedonian is the official language of the country, very similar to Bulgarian and Serbo-Croatian spoken in neighboring countries. The language is formally written in Cyrillic, but many street signs are also written in the Latin alphabet. Most people speak good English, so it's easy to get around for travelers. Out in the villages it helps to know a word or two of Macedonian, and zdravo (hello) goes a long way. Both Albanian and Turkish are also spoken by ethnic minorities throughout the country.
